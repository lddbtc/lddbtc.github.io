Field notes for importers

Two short notes for buyers placing a first order with a supplier they have never met. Written as working checklists, not as a sales pitch.

Note 1 — Is the "factory" a real factory?

A valid business licence proves almost nothing on its own, because a trading company can have a perfectly real registration. Check the scope line: does it include actual production of the product you are buying, and is the registered address the same site as the workshop?

Ask for a photo of the running line taken that day, with the date visible and machine nameplates in the same frame, plus a recent production record compared with the capacity number in the quotation. A staged still and a brochure say nothing about capacity. Also confirm that the company name on the invoice, packing list and bank beneficiary all match the licence; a mismatch usually means you are paying an intermediary.

Note 2 — Structuring the first order

The deposit is not the risky part by itself. The risky part is releasing the balance against evidence the seller produced. A pre-shipment check only has value when it is done on the finished lot, after production, by someone who is not paid by the factory.

Write acceptance criteria into the purchase order in words and numbers (quantity tolerance, workmanship, packaging, labels, carton marks), keep a signed and sealed golden sample, and release the balance only after a report on the actual lot is delivered. On loading day, record the container seal number and photograph the outer carton marks: short shipments and mixed SKUs usually start at the loading bay.
