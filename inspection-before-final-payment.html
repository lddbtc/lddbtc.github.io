Inspect Before Final Payment: Your Last Point of Leverage

Once the balance is wired, your leverage is gone. A supplier who has been paid in full has very little commercial reason to rework defects, replace materials or hold a shipment. The single most effective protection available to an overseas buyer is a contractual rule: no balance payment until an independent inspection passes.

Why 'pay first, fix later' rarely works

Remedies after shipment are expensive and slow. Returning a container, filing a dispute on a platform, or claiming against a Chinese entity from abroad can cost more than the goods. Rework after the goods arrive in your market is worse: you pay freight, duty and storage on defective stock.

The practical answer is to keep the final 30–70% unpaid until the goods are finished, packed and checked. This is standard in well-run sourcing operations and is not an unreasonable demand of a professional supplier.

Write it into the contract, not just an email

Include four clauses in the purchase order or contract: (1) the AQL or acceptance standard that applies; (2) who pays for the inspection and the re-inspection; (3) that final payment is released only after a written pass report from an independent inspector; (4) that the supplier must allow access to the finished goods and packing area before shipment.

Specify the consequences of failure too — rework at the supplier's cost, a re-inspection, and a defined deadline. Ambiguity here is what turns a failed inspection into a three-week negotiation. For what the check itself covers, see the pre-shipment inspection checklist.

What counts as a pass

A pass is not 'the samples looked fine'. It is a written report with the quantity checked, workmanship findings measured against the agreed standard, dimensions and specification compliance, packaging and labelling, photos with timestamps, and a clear verdict: pass, conditional or fail.

Ask for the report before you release funds, and read the defect counts rather than only the conclusion. If you need to know what a professional report contains, see what a real inspection report should contain.

When the supplier pushes back

Common objections: 'our QC already checked', 'the container must be booked today', 'inspection will delay shipment'. A factory's internal QC is not independent — it reports to the same person who needs the shipment to leave. Booking pressure is a scheduling issue, not a quality argument; book the inspection slot when production reaches 80%.

If a supplier insists on full payment before any independent check, either reduce the order to an amount you can afford to lose, or find another supplier. Review the cost of a pre-shipment inspection against the size of the balance you are protecting.
