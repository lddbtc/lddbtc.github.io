Pre-shipment inspection cost in China (2026)

Third-party inspection in China is almost always priced per man-day — one inspector, one day, one city. Understanding what moves that number stops you over-paying and stops you skipping the check that would have saved a container of rejects.

How inspection is priced

Most independent inspectors in China quote a flat man-day rate in USD, covering the inspector's time, on-site work, the report and photos. Travel within a major cluster is usually included; remote cities add a travel day. You are not paying per unit, per container or per defect — you are paying for a qualified person's day on the floor.

What actually changes the price

  • Sampling standard (AQL level). A tighter AQL (e.g. 1.0 vs 2.5) means more samples and more time. I set the level with you before I go, not after.
  • Product complexity. A simple woven bag is faster than a wired appliance with function tests. Complexity drives how many units get physically tested.
  • City / cluster. Inspections inside the main manufacturing belts (Shandong, Yangtze, Pearl River Delta) cost less than a single factory in a remote inland province.
  • Man-days. One day covers most shipments. Large orders, multiple lines or re-inspection after a fail add days — agreed up front.

Why the cost is almost always worth it

A single man-day costs less than the freight on one returned container, let alone the lost season, the chargeback or the ruined listing. The inspection checklist exists to catch the defects before they are on a ship. Buyers who skip it are not saving money — they are betting the whole shipment.

What a fixed USD quote should include

Insist on a quote that names the man-day rate, what is included (report, photos, AQL level, city), travel terms, and the fail/re-inspection policy. No "we'll see after I'm there."
