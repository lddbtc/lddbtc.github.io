How to avoid a Chinese supplier scam

Most "scams" are not criminals in a basement — they are trading companies dressed as factories, or real factories that quietly subcontract your order. Neither shows up until you have wired the deposit. These seven checks catch both before money moves.

Home › Avoid supplier scams

1. A "factory" with no factory address

If the only address you are given is a sales office, co-working space or a WeChat location pin, treat it as a trader until proved otherwise. Ask for the production site address and the registered address on the business licence — and have someone stand at the gate. A 4-desk office is not where your goods are made.

2. The business licence scope says "trading"

A perfectly real registration proves little. The tell is the scope line: does it list actual manufacturing of your product, or only "sales / trading / import-export"? A trading company can own a licence and a website and still have zero machines. Check this before anything else — see the full factory verification guide.

3. Certificate photos that do not match

CE, ISO 9001, UL, SGS — they are copied and Photoshopped daily. The check is provenance: is the certificate issued to this company name, at this address, for this product scope, and still in date? I photograph the original document on site, with the licence beside it, so you can verify the number yourself.

4. They vanish the moment the deposit lands

The classic pattern: fast replies, eager video calls, then silence after the TT transfer. A real supplier welcomes a third-party point of contact and a pre-shipment inspection written into the contract. If inspection is "not necessary" or "we never allow it," that is your answer.

5. Perfect sample, different mass production

Scammers send a genuine sample sourced from a competitor, then fulfil with a cheap substitute. The only reliable fix is to inspect the actual production run against the approved sample, with AQL sampling and on-site photos of the real batch.

6. A price that is too good to be real

A quote 30–50% under the market is rarely a bargain — it is a bait. Either the spec is quietly lower, the material is substituted, or the "supplier" never intended to ship. Get a fixed USD quote that names the exact specification, and compare like-for-like.

7. They refuse any independent inspection or visit

This is the single highest-signal red flag. A real plant has nothing to hide from a buyer-paid inspector. If a supplier blocks a factory audit or pre-shipment check, walk away — or order a Supplier Reality Check and let me go anyway.

The pattern behind every scam

None of these flags alone proves dishonesty. The danger is the stack: no real address + licence-says-trading + refuses inspection + too-good price. When two or more line up, do not wire. Send me the supplier's address and licence and I will tell you what is actually there.
