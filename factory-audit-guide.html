A factory audit answers one question a certificate cannot: can this specific plant actually make your product, legally and consistently? Here is what a real audit covers.

Two kinds of audit — know which you need

🔧 Capability audit

Does the plant have the equipment, workforce and track record to make your product? This is what most first-time buyers actually need, and what catches trading fronts.

⚖️ Social-compliance audit

Labour, safety, environmental and ethical standards (SA8000, BSCI, Sedex/SMETA). Needed for EU/US retailers and branded goods; often a separate, longer engagement.

What a capability audit checks

  • Legal entity — business licence original, registered address, and whether scope includes real production.
  • Premises & equipment — workshop, machine nameplates, and whether the line runs that day.
  • Workforce — headcount, shifts, and skilled vs temporary labour.
  • Capacity — line count and recent production records vs the quoted number.
  • Quality system — in-process checks, calibration, incoming-material control, traceability.
  • Export experience — prior shipments to your market, incoterms handled, documentation discipline.

Reading the report without being fooled

  • Watch the licence scope — "trading" scope with a pretty workshop photo is still a trader.
  • Watch capacity vs pitch — a 200-worker claim with a 20-person floor is a red flag.
  • Watch certificate ownership — the cert must belong to the audited legal entity, not a sister company.
