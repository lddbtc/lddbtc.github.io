How to choose a third-party inspection company in China

"Third-party" is a label, not a guarantee. The only question that matters is simple: when the report is written, whose interest does the inspector protect — yours, or the supplier's? Here is how to tell before you book.

Home › Choose an inspector

1. Who pays the inspector

The fastest test: is the inspector paid by you, the buyer, or by the factory? If the supplier "arranges inspection for free," the inspector's next job depends on the supplier's mood, not your cargo. Always book and pay direct. See why independence is the whole point.

2. Affiliated vs independent

Large international brands and small local inspectors can both be independent — and both can be captured. What separates them is the incentive structure, not the logo. A local, buyer-paid inspector with same-day access to the cluster often catches more than a distant auditor flown in for two hours. Ask what an audit actually covers and judge by the checklist, not the brand.

3. Local response time

Inspection is only useful if it can happen the day production finishes. An inspector based in the manufacturing belt (Shandong, Yangtze, Pearl River Delta) reaches the factory in hours; one flying in from another city adds a travel day and a travel fee. For the Shandong clusters, same-day access is the default, not a premium.

4. Report standard you can forward

A good report states the AQL level used, the sample size, the defects found, timestamped photos and an honest pass/fail verdict — formatted so you can send it to your team or your bank. Vague "quality is good" write-ups are worthless. Insist on a structured report before you book.

5. The right size for your order

For most SME importers, a big-brand inspection is overpriced overhead; a solo buyer-paid local inspector is lean and direct. For regulated PPE or food contact, verify the inspector understands the certification evidence (see the glove audit example). Match the inspector to the risk, not to the invoice size.

The one-line rule

If you cannot say "this inspector is paid by me and answers to me," you do not have third-party inspection — you have a courtesy visit the factory scheduled. Book a buyer-paid inspection and the answer is always you.
