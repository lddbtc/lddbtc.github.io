What a real glove plant shows you

  • Knitting machines. The liner line (13-gauge, 15-gauge, cut-resistant yarns) running your SKU — not a display rack.
  • Dipping line. Latex / nitrile / PU coating, with cure ovens and the batch records. Coating consistency is where subcontracting shows.
  • CE / PPE marking. The EU 2016/425 PPE regulation requires a valid Declaration of Conformity and an EU authorised representative. The document must name this factory, not a trading中间商.
  • Test reports. EN388 (mechanical risks) and any EN374/EN407 issued by a notified body, for the exact glove model, in date.

The certification trap

Many Gaomi "manufacturers" resell gloves made by a handful of real plants and borrow those plants' CE files. That is not conformance — it is documentation laundering. The audit must confirm the tested model matches the produced model and the DoC names the supplier you are paying. See the red-flag guide for the licence-scope check that exposes this.

What I verify on a glove audit

I photograph the knitting and dipping lines running your glove, check the licence scope covers production, and read the CE / EN388 documents on site — issuer, model, date, and whether the EU rep is real. If the supplier blocks floor access, that itself is the finding. Follow it with a pre-shipment check on the actual shipment.
