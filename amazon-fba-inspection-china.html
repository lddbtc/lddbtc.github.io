Pre-Shipment Inspection for Amazon FBA Sellers

For an FBA seller, a quality problem is not just a defective unit — it is a rejected shipment, a stranded inventory fee, a suppressed listing or a wave of returns. Amazon's requirements are unforgiving and mostly administrative. The good news: nearly all of them can be checked at the factory before the goods ship.

The FBA-specific checks that belong on the checklist

FNSKU or other unit labels: correct code, scannable, applied to the correct sellable unit, positioned so the barcode is flat and unobstructed. Polybags: suffocation warning present and legible, transparent bag, sealed, correct thickness, no mixed SKUs in one bag. Cartons: correct dimensions and weight limits, no damaged corners, no staples or strapping that could injure a receiving associate, and the shipment carton labels applied.

Also check the unit count per carton against the shipment plan, the expiration or lot labels where relevant, and that the packaging you approved is the packaging being used. A surprising number of FBA problems are a labelling or count error, not a manufacturing defect.

Where generic inspection falls short

A standard pre-shipment inspection focuses on workmanship, function, appearance and packaging. It may never scan a barcode or read a suffocation warning. When you book an inspection, give the inspector your exact FBA requirements in writing and ask for photographs of the label itself, a scan test and the carton markings.

Ask for a labelled-photo set: unit label close-up, warning text close-up, inner pack, carton interior, carton exterior with the shipment label. These five photos resolve most disputes about what was shipped. The general framework is in the pre-shipment inspection checklist — add your FBA clauses to it.

Timing and quantity

Inspect when at least 80% of the order is produced and packed, and before the supplier books the container or hands goods to a freight forwarder. If goods are being labelled by a third-party prep centre in China rather than the factory, inspect at that location instead — inspecting the wrong site is a common and expensive mistake.

For a first order with a new supplier, add a factory verification before production starts. It is much cheaper to discover that the 'factory' is a trading company before you print 5,000 FNSKU labels.

Make it part of the payment rule

Keep the balance conditional on a pass report that explicitly includes your FBA requirements. If labelling fails, re-labelling at the factory is a same-day fix; re-labelling at an Amazon fulfilment centre is a removal order and weeks of lost sales.

Small sellers are often told inspection is not worth it for a small shipment. Compare the fee with the cost of one rejected carton and a suppressed listing — see what pre-shipment inspection costs.
