How to verify a Chinese factory is real

A valid business licence proves almost nothing on its own. These are the six checks that actually separate a real plant from a trading front — and all six need a person standing at the gate.

Home › Verify a factory

1. Business licence scope, not just the licence

A trading company can have a perfectly real registration. The tell is the scope line: does it include actual production of the product you are buying, or only "sales / trading"? I check the original licence against the registered address — and whether that address is the same building as the workshop.

2. Real premises and equipment

Factory gate, workshop, machine nameplates, workers on shift, and whether the line is actually running that day. A rented office with four desks is not a factory. Ask for a photo of the running line taken that day, with the date visible and machine nameplates in the same frame.

3. Capacity evidence vs the sales pitch

Line count, shifts and recent production records, compared against the capacity number in the quotation. A staged still and a brochure say nothing about capacity. I ask for a recent production record and read the real one, not the one prepared for the visit.

4. Certificate originals

CE / EN388 / API / UL-FM / FDA / HACCP / DoP originals, checked against the issuing body and the exact legal entity. A supplier showing a certificate that belongs to a different company is a classic front.

5. Document consistency

Invoice, packing list, bill of lading and bank beneficiary must point to the same entity. Mismatches are the classic trading-front tell — and the reason an independent inspector matters more than a friendly one.

6. Trader → real factory trace-back

If your "supplier" is a trading company, I trace it back to the actual OEM factory and audit that factory separately before you pay. You are buying from the plant, so the plant is what must be verified.
