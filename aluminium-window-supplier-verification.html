Verifying an aluminium window & door supplier in China

Linqu, in Shandong, is one of China's densest aluminium profile and window-system clusters — which means it is also dense with traders reselling the same dozen real factories. Verification here is less about "is it real" and more about "which real factory is actually making yours."

What a real window/door plant has on site

  • Extrusion line. Presses, die library and the profile cross-section your order uses. A trader shows you a catalogue; a plant shows you the press running your die.
  • Surface treatment. Anodising or powder-coating in-house (or a named, verifiable sub-contractor). Coating quality is where cheap quotes cut corners.
  • Assembly & glazing. Cutting, corner-crimping, hardware fitting, sealed glazing unit assembly. Ask to see your exact system being built.
  • Test equipment. Profilometer, coating-thickness gauge, sealant cure records. Real plants measure; traders describe.

Documents that actually matter

Beyond the business licence, ask for the extrusion licence scope, the coating line environmental permit, and any EN/ISO or Chinese GB test reports issued to this company for this profile series. Photocopied reports from an unrelated plant are the most common fake in this cluster.

Trader traps specific to this cluster

Linqu has hundreds of "manufacturers" with a showroom and a WeChat catalogue but no press. The tell is simple: can they let an inspector onto the extrusion floor? If the visit is "warehouse only" or "office only," you are buying from a trader. See the scam red-flag list for the full pattern.

What I check on a window/door audit
