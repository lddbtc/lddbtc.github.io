Container Loading Supervision: The Last Chance Before the Gate Closes

A perfect pre-shipment inspection does not help if the wrong cartons go into the container. Loading supervision covers the few hours when goods leave the factory: quantity, carton condition, loading method, container integrity and the seal. It is the cheapest insurance against short shipments, mixed SKUs and water damage.

What the supervisor verifies on site

Before loading: the container number, the physical condition of the container (roof, walls, floor, doors, holes, rust, odour, moisture), and whether the container is dry and free of previous cargo residue. A photograph of the empty container interior is essential evidence if a damage claim follows.

During loading: carton count against the packing list, carton condition, correct SKUs and markings, no mixed or substituted cartons, sensible stacking and dunnage, and no loading in the rain. After loading: the seal is applied in view of the supervisor, the seal number is photographed and recorded on the report, and the container number is cross-checked against the booking.

The problems this catches

Short loading — cartons held back because production ran short. Substitution — older stock or a different specification loaded after the inspection. Mixed SKUs — cartons with the same outer marking but different contents. Wet or damaged cartons accepted by the loading crew. And, occasionally, a container with a hole in the roof that turns a twenty-day sea voyage into a total loss.

These are not theoretical. They are routine, and almost all of them are invisible once the container is sealed and trucked to port.

What you receive

A loading supervision report with the container and seal numbers, time stamps for arrival, loading start and finish, the count of cartons loaded, weather and conditions, any deviations, and a photo set: empty container, first layers, part-loaded, full load, closed doors, seal in place, and the truck departing.

That evidence trail is what makes a claim possible. Without it, a short-shipment dispute becomes one party's word against another's. Pair this with the pre-shipment inspection so the goods are checked and the loading is checked.

When to use it

Loading supervision is most valuable for first orders with a new supplier, high-value or fragile goods, multi-SKU containers, orders where the factory loads at a third-party warehouse, and any shipment where the balance is paid before the container leaves.
