How to Verify an Alibaba Supplier Before You Pay

A supplier profile on a marketplace is marketing, not evidence. Gold badges, years listed and transaction counts describe an account, not the factory that will actually make your goods. Before any deposit leaves your account, verify the legal entity, the physical premises and the ownership of the bank account you are paying.

1. Verify the legal entity, not the storefront

Ask for the Chinese business licence (营业执照) and the exact registered company name in Chinese. Check three things: the unified social credit code, the registered address, and whether the business scope actually covers manufacturing your product. A company whose scope says 'trading, import and export' but not 'manufacturing' is not a factory.

Cross-check the name against the name on the proforma invoice and the beneficiary name on the bank details. If the invoice comes from 'Weifang XX Trading Co., Ltd.' but the bank account is in the name of an individual or a different company, stop and ask why. This mismatch is one of the most common precursors to a payment that cannot be recovered.

2. Confirm the factory physically exists

Marketplace photos are frequently reused. Ask for a live video walk-through at a specific time, showing the street sign, the gate with the company name, the production line running your type of product, and the warehouse. Then have someone visit in person.

An on-site visit answers the questions a video cannot: is the workshop rented for the day, are the machines theirs, how many lines are actually running, and do the workers, raw materials and finished goods match the claimed capacity? This is the core of a factory verification visit.

3. Watch for the trading-company layer

A trading company is not automatically a scam — many are competent and useful. The risk is not knowing which one you are dealing with, and paying a markup while having no control over the workshop that sets your quality.

Ask directly: will the goods be produced at the address on the licence? If the answer is vague, request the name and address of the actual production site, then verify that site separately. Also ask who controls the QC process and who signs the inspection report.

4. Lock verification into your payment terms

Make the deposit conditional on a satisfactory verification, and keep the balance conditional on a pre-shipment inspection. Write into the purchase contract that final payment is released only after an independent inspection passes.

If a supplier refuses any independent check of the premises or the goods before shipment, treat that refusal as the answer. See also how to avoid Chinese supplier scams for the full red-flag list.
